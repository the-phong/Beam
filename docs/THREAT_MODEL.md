# Experimental threat model

The defender is a private four-validator CometBFT/Pluto network. An arbitrary sender may submit malformed or oversized bytes to allowlisted RPC; a signed-transaction modifier may mutate observed proof bytes; a registered key holder may create distinct valid or intentionally state-invalid transfers. None controls validator keys, consensus rules, the fixed genesis registry or measurement oracle. The study did not exercise independent malicious peers or P2P-origin injection.

The mechanisms are distinct: early precheck/cache rejection, application signature verification before rejection, accepted mempool byte/count pressure, P2P relay of accepted transactions, and proposal/commit work. A rejected CheckTx transaction is not accepted mempool occupancy. A scenario label describes how a workload was generated, not a conclusion from the rejection code. Ordinary user bursts and natural client errors may be benign.

A DoS claim would require matched legitimate-service degradation and credible saturation/causal evidence. The observed 3-second endpoint stayed negative in the tested matched single-host RPC strata; X10 showed a continuous upper-bound latency difference within its registered relevance margin, while X13 found no knee through the safe cap. No DoS, distributed susceptibility or immunity claim follows.
