# D-026 operator recovery for an incomplete B0 home

An `init-b0` error or interrupted process leaves the home untouched for inspection. `start` requires an existing integrity-valid B0 committed snapshot and matching Pluto/Comet genesis metadata; it never creates H0 or resets an old state. Repeating `init-b0` on the same path is refused.

1. Stop Pluto and CometBFT. Preserve a byte-for-byte copy of the entire home, the command line, genesis binary supplied to `init-b0`, process logs and filesystem error reports before attempting recovery. Keep validator keys and `priv_validator_state.json` private. Record file hashes and timestamps without editing the original.
2. Inspect `config/pluto.json`, `config/genesis.json`, `config/config.toml`, `config/priv_validator_key.json`, `config/node_key.json`, `data/priv_validator_state.json`, `data/pluto-b0-v2`, and any CometBFT `state.db` and `blockstore.db`. A missing `pluto.json` means initialization did not complete. A missing or unreadable B0 committed record means `start` must refuse the home. A valid H0 record alone does not prove all configuration files were durably synchronized.
3. Verify the expected chain ID and canonical Genesis V2 binary from an independent trusted source. Compare the D-025 JSON/Base64 `genesis.json` transport and Pluto metadata digest with that binary. Confirm that H0 has the expected AppHash, account balances, nonce zero and supply; for later heights, inspect Comet's matching-height consensus AppHash and replay availability. Do not compare hashes from different heights.
4. If any identity, genesis, height, AppHash or replay-history fact conflicts, retain the copy and stop for Controller/operator review. Do not edit the B0 record, remove Comet data, replace validator state, or use `init-b0` in place. A validator key and its last-sign state are a safety pair; careless reuse or rollback can double sign.
5. On an isolated disposable experimental home only, after preserving evidence, the operator may choose a **new path** and run `init-b0` with independently verified Genesis V2 and chain ID. This is a new initialization, not automatic repair of the old home. Reusing an existing validator identity requires separate manual assessment of chain history and last-sign state.

When both B0 and Comet data are absent but configuration remains, `start` refuses the missing H0. When the entire home and every local trace have been erased, software cannot prove whether a blockchain existed there previously. SIGKILL and simulated I/O errors exercise process-level recovery; they do not establish physical power-loss or device-cache durability, especially on WSL2/9p.
